Full Product Plan v3

OVERVIEW

Socrify is a Socratic AI tutoring platform for students and teachers across a wide range of subjects. It uses guided questioning over direct answers to build genuine understanding. Monetized via subscriptions and ads, with a dedicated teacher/class management system.

Domain: socrify.pro (beta) → socrify.ai (full release)

**Stack:** React · Anthropic API (Claude Haiku 4.5 / Sonnet for Practice + Teacher tools) · Supabase · Netlify (serverless proxy) · Stripe · AdSense

-----

## MODES

Five distinct modes — not re-prompted chat. Each has its own UI layout.

-----

### 1. Tutor Mode

**Core rule: Never gives direct answers. Ever. On any tier.**

- Adapts wording, teaching style, analogies, and pacing based on the student.

- Session prompt injection based on student's onboarding profile (grade, interests, strengths, experience).

- Uses the Socratic method: asks leading questions, prompts reflection, redirects wrong thinking.

- If a student is completely stuck: they press a "I'm stuck" button. AI asks them to explain where they got lost, then rephrases, gives a hint, or approaches from a different angle — still no direct answer.

- Jailbreak resistance baked into system prompt. AI is instructed to never give a direct answer regardless of how the question is phrased, wrapped, or roleplay-framed.

- Different subject-level personas via prompt variation (e.g. methodical for Math, narrative for History) — same model, different system prompt flavor.

- **UI:** Chat-style, but AI responses render as distinct "question cards" — not plain chat bubbles.

-----

### 2. Research Mode

Deep topic exploration, AI-led and structured.

- AI suggests angles, related concepts, follow-up questions, and rabbit holes.

- Encourages the student to form their own thesis or conclusions — never writes it for them.

- Great for essay prep, project research, curiosity-driven learning.

- **UI:** Split layout — AI guidance panel on the left, student scratchpad/notes on the right. More focused and tool-like, not a chatbot.

-----

### 3. Idea Mode

Brainstorms essay topics, project ideas, content ideas, business ideas.

- AI generates a set of idea cards for the student to evaluate, explore, or reject.

- **Re-roll button** — regenerates a fresh batch of ideas with one click.

- Helps develop creative thinking and decision-making, not just outputs.

- **UI:** Card grid layout. Each card is an idea with a short description. Student taps to expand or go deeper. Not a chat layout.

-----

### 4. Practice Mode

Structured quiz UI — no chat bubbles at all.

- **Session starts with a prompt:** Student describes the topic they want to practice, then picks Quick Quiz or Long Quiz.

- Questions generated dynamically using **Claude Sonnet** based on the topic and student's difficulty level.

- Question types: multiple choice, short answer, fill-in-the-blank (subject-dependent).

- Coding challenges with built-in code editor (similar to W3Schools / Mimo) for CS/coding subjects.

- Typing drills UI for typing subject.

- **"Explain My Mistake"** button after a wrong answer — triggers a Socratic explanation of why that answer was wrong without revealing the correct one.

- Score tracking within session.

- Adaptive difficulty memory — tracks performance over time and nudges difficulty automatically.

- **UI:** Progress bar, answer input, score counter. Clean quiz layout, not conversational.

-----

### 5. Grader / Reviewer Mode

Grade and review work the student has already made.

- Student pastes or uploads an essay or project writeup.

- AI grades using a structured rubric displayed as a scorecard.

- **Essay rubric (out of 5):**

  - Structure — /3 (ideas, structure, explanation)

  - Grammar & Spelling — /2

  - Total: /5

  - Based on the Texas 4/5-point writing rubric (widely used, publicly available).

- AI feedback is constructive, not harsh. Encourages revision.

- **UI:** Upload/paste area → scorecard breakdown → written feedback panel.

-----

## SESSION FLOW

At the start of every session (all modes):

1. Select subject (from grid — previously used subjects shown first, + explore more)

2. Select mode (with one-line descriptions for new users)

3. Grade level and difficulty are pre-filled from profile — student can adjust per session

**Session History (Free tier excluded, Guests excluded):**

- Full transcript saved per session

- Browsable by date and subject

- Searchable

- PDF export (Pro+)

- Students in a class cannot delete class-session transcripts. Private sessions can be deleted.

**Session Summary:**

At the end of every session, AI generates a 3–5 bullet "what you worked on today" card, saved with the transcript.

## UI LAYOUT

### Overall Structure

Dashboard-first layout (similar to Duolingo / Khan Academy). Collapsible sidebar for navigation.

- **Ad sidebar** — visible to Guest and Free users only. Completely absent (not just hidden) for Pro, Teacher, Teacher Pro, class students, and all Enterprise users.

- The ad sidebar component should be conditionally rendered based on org_id or class_id presence, not just tier — so a student who joins a class mid-session doesn't see ads bleed through from a prior state.

- Ad layout inspired by chess.com — right-side panel, doesn't interrupt content flow.

- Subject picker: icon + label cards in a grid. Previously used subjects shown first. "+ Explore more" expands all available subjects.

- Mode selector appears after subject pick — each mode shows a one-line description.

- **Sidebar ads** — Guest + Free tier — AdSense (placeholder during beta)

- **Rewarded ads** — Free tier — Watch ad → earn tokens (desktop delivery TBD, likely AdSense rewarded or IronSource)

- **No ads** — Pro, Teacher, Teacher Pro, Enterprise

- 1 rewarded ad = 5 tokens

- 1 extra session = 10 tokens

- Tokens stored in Supabase on user record

- Tokens carry over to Pro upgrade but become irrelevant (unlimited sessions)

- Token balance always visible in UI

- Tokens don't expire

- No ID or face verification required

- School email domain verification (e.g. .edu, .k12.tx.us, recognized district domains)

- Optional: self-attestation checkbox ("I confirm I'm an educator at this institution")

- Stripe billing acts as passive friction — unlikely bad actors pay $10–20/mo

- Abuse can be addressed reactively if needed; tighten verification later if patterns emerge

Beta

BETA BUILD PLAN — socrify.pro

Beta is scoped to prove the core concept: that Socratic AI tutoring is genuinely useful and different from everything else out there. No teacher system, no enterprise, no payment infrastructure. Just the student-facing product, stable, working, and good enough to share.

Domain: socrify.pro

Tiers in beta: Guest and Free only. No Stripe, no Pro, no Teacher, no Enterprise.

Modes in beta: Tutor Mode and Research Mode. Practice, Idea, and Grader come in v1.0.

AI model: Claude Haiku 4.5 for tutor and Haiku 4.5 on extended thinking for research.

Auth & User Accounts

Use Supabase Auth with username and password only. No email required at signup — email is optional and only used for password reset. No OAuth, no magic links in beta.

Guest users: no account, no Supabase record. Drop them straight into subject selection. Rate limit by IP. Session data is ephemeral — nothing saved.

Free users: Supabase users table row. Store username, first name, grade level, interests, strengths, experience (from onboarding), and is_dev boolean. No session history in beta — that’s a v1.0 feature.

is_dev flag: Boolean on the users table. When true, bypass all rate limits and tier checks. Used for internal testing. Never expose this in UI.

Onboarding Flow

Run every new Free account through onboarding before they reach the dashboard. One step per screen, progress bar visible. Do not present this as a wall of form fields.

Step 1 — Username

Step 2 — Password

Step 3 — First name (last name optional)

Step 4 — Grade level

Step 5 — Interests (multi-select tags: Gaming, Music, Sports, Tech, etc.)

Step 6 — What you’re good at (multi-select: Math, Writing, Science, etc.)

Step 7 — Experiences (open text or tags: “took AP Bio”, “learning guitar”, “run a small business”)

Save all onboarding data to the users table. This data is injected into the AI system prompt on every session to personalize analogies, pacing, and topic connections.

Tiers (Beta)

Guest — no account

Access all subjects and both beta modes. No session history. Hourly AI call rate limit (IP-based). Sidebar ad placeholder layout visible but ads not live in beta. Extreme session limits.

Free — account required

4 sessions per day. Hourly rate limits. All beta subjects and modes. Sidebar ad placeholder layout present. No session history in beta. Onboarding profile used to personalize AI.

Dev (is_dev = true)

No rate limits, no session caps. Full access to everything. Used for internal testing only.

Session Flow (Beta)

1. Student selects a subject from the subject grid. Previously used subjects appear first. “+ Explore more” expands the full list.

2. Student selects a mode. Each mode shows a one-line description for new users. Beta exposes Tutor Mode and Research Mode only.

3. Grade level and difficulty are pre-filled from the user’s profile. Student can adjust per session before starting.

4. Session begins. The AI system prompt is assembled from: mode identity, subject context, student’s onboarding profile, and jailbreak-resistance instructions.

No session history is saved in beta. No session summary card. Those come in v1.0.

Subjects (Beta Launch)

Scope to the academic core for beta. Do not build subject-specific UI variations yet — the AI system prompt handles subject persona differences.

Math / Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics) / History / Geography / English & Literature / Computer Science Fundamentals / Essay Writing

Essay Writing note: Socrify helps with structure, feedback, and organization only. It does not write the essay. It will not give the student content to paste in. This constraint must be enforced in the system prompt, not just the UI.

Tutor Mode — Beta Implementation

Core rule, non-negotiable: the AI never gives a direct answer. Not in beta, not in any tier, not ever. This is the product. Bake jailbreak resistance into the system prompt. The AI must refuse to answer directly regardless of how the question is phrased, roleplay-framed, or wrapped.

UI layout: Chat-style, but AI responses render as distinct question cards — not plain chat bubbles. Do not use a standard chat message component for AI output.

“I’m Stuck” button: Always visible. When pressed, the AI asks the student to describe where they got lost, then rephrases or approaches from a different angle. Still no direct answer.

Subject personas: Vary the system prompt tone by subject. Math = methodical, step-by-step. History = narrative, context-heavy. Science = evidence-first. Same model, different prompt flavor. Do not hard-code these as separate components — handle via prompt injection.

Profile injection: Pull the student’s grade, interests, strengths, and experiences from their profile and include them in the system prompt. The AI uses these to choose better analogies and gauge pacing.

Research Mode — Beta Implementation

UI layout: Split layout. AI guidance panel on the left. Student scratchpad on the right. This is a tool, not a chatbot. Do not default to a single-column chat layout.

AI behavior: Suggest angles, related concepts, follow-up questions, and rabbit holes. Never write the student’s thesis or conclusions for them. Push them toward forming their own position.

Scratchpad is a freeform text area. Student notes are client-side only in beta — not persisted to Supabase. Export Notes (to Google Docs or PDF) is a post-beta feature.

API & Infrastructure

Netlify Functions proxy: All Anthropic API calls must go through a Netlify serverless function. Never call the Anthropic API directly from the client. The function holds the API key server-side and proxies the request. This is mandatory for CORS and key security.

Model: claude-haiku-4-5 for all beta sessions. Do not use Sonnet in beta — cost control is critical at this stage.

Rate limiting: Enforce hourly limits on Guest and Free at the serverless function level, not just the frontend. Frontend checks are UI sugar — the function must reject requests that exceed limits regardless of what the client sends.

Supabase: Auth + users table only in beta. No sessions table, no transcripts table, no teacher/class tables. Keep the schema minimal.

UI & Design

Framework: React. Font: Montserrat. Styling: Tailwind or CSS modules.

Default theme: Light blue. Dark mode available. OLED black is post-beta (Pro+ only).

Dashboard layout: Collapsible sidebar for navigation. Subject picker is a card grid with icon + label. Ad sidebar placeholder is rendered in the layout for Guest and Free but contains no live ads in beta.

Dyslexia font toggle: Available to all users including Guest. Accessible from settings or a visible toggle. Use OpenDyslexic or similar.

Target feel: Professional enough that a teacher or school administrator wouldn’t immediately dismiss it. Approachable enough that a middle schooler doesn’t feel like they’re in a corporate tool.

What Beta Explicitly Does NOT Include

Do not build any of the following for beta. They are confirmed for v1.0 or later. Building them now will bloat scope and delay launch.

Practice Mode, Idea Mode, Grader Mode

Pro tier, Stripe billing, any payment flow

Teacher tier, Teacher Pro tier, Enterprise, class system, join codes

Session history, transcript export, PDF export, session summary cards

Live AdSense ads, rewarded ads, token system

Daily streaks, subject mastery levels, referral system, Study Path

OLED black theme, expanded theme customization

Claude Sonnet (cost too high for unmonetized beta)

Post-launch subjects: Coding, Typing, Music Theory, Language Learning, Finance, etc.

Beta Success Criteria

Beta is done when: auth works reliably, onboarding saves correctly, Tutor Mode and Research Mode run without breaking, the AI stays in character and never gives direct answers, rate limits actually enforce at the serverless layer, and the product is stable enough to share with real students without hand-holding.

Exit criteria for beta → v1.0: at least a small group of real users has used it and found it genuinely useful. Gather that signal, then build the rest.

Features

FEATURES (and more modes)

Tutor Mode

“Flag for Teacher” escalation

Add a “Flag for Teacher” button alongside the existing “I’m Stuck” button, visible only to students enrolled in a class. When a student has gone through multiple AI rephrasing attempts and is still not making progress, they flag it. This creates a notification in the teacher’s dashboard so the teacher can follow up directly. Implement as a flagged_sessions record in Supabase tied to the student’s user ID and class ID.

Breadcrumb citations in hints

When the AI gives a hint in Tutor Mode, it must also include a specific reference to material the student has uploaded or a concept from the current session. A hint is not just a Socratic question — it points the student somewhere concrete. Example format in the system prompt: “After your question, include one sentence directing the student to a specific section of their notes or uploaded material.” If no material has been uploaded, reference a concept already discussed in the session.

Curriculum-locked context mode

Allow students to upload class materials (PDF, Google Doc link, or pasted text) before starting a Tutor session. When materials are present, inject them into the system prompt context window and instruct the AI to restrict all hints and questions to the uploaded content only. The AI must not bring in outside information, even if that information is accurate. This prevents the AI from contradicting the teacher’s specific framing of a topic.

Research Mode

Export scratchpad notes

Add an “Export Notes” button to the scratchpad panel. Target options: download as PDF, or push to Google Docs via the Google Docs API (OAuth required). The PDF path is simpler and should ship first. Google Docs integration is secondary. Export is available to Pro users; Free users see the button but are prompted to upgrade. Consistent with how transcript PDF export is gated elsewhere in the product.

Active Study Guide from uploaded materials

When a student uploads a chapter or notes, do not generate a summary. Generate an Active Study Guide instead: a mix of targeted questions, fill-in-the-blank exercises, and matching prompts drawn directly from the uploaded text. The AI is explicitly instructed not to summarize — its only output in this flow is the study guide in structured format. Render it in the right panel as a formatted, interactive document, not a chat message.

Visual mindmap from notes

Add a “Generate Mindmap” button in Research Mode. The AI parses the student’s notes or uploaded content and returns a structured node/edge JSON. Render it as an interactive mindmap using a library like react-flow or vis.js. Each node is clickable and expands a short concept summary inline. This gives visual learners a scannable overview before diving into the scratchpad. Mindmap generation is a separate API call from the main session — trigger it explicitly, not automatically.

Idea Mode

Pin cards before re-rolling

Each idea card in the grid has a pin icon. Pinned cards are held in place when the student hits Re-roll — only unpinned cards are replaced. Pinned cards move to the top of the grid. Store pinned state in component state, not Supabase — this is a within-session feature. Pinned cards are cleared when the session ends or the student navigates away.

Voice-to-text brainstorm input

Add a microphone button to Idea Mode. The student presses it and speaks freely about what they think their project should be about — no structure required. Use the Web Speech API (browser-native, no third-party cost) to transcribe. Send the raw transcript to the AI with instructions to: (1) extract the student’s core intent, (2) identify gaps in their thinking, and (3) generate idea cards based on what they actually described rather than a generic prompt. The AI response feeds directly into the card grid, same as a typed prompt.

Practice Mode

Socratic wrong-answer coaching

When a student gets a question wrong, the existing “Explain My Mistake” flow triggers a Socratic explanation. Extend this: the AI must never reveal the correct answer during this coaching step. It asks the student to reconsider their reasoning, points out which part of their logic broke down, and gives one additional hint. The student can attempt the question again after coaching. Only reveal the correct answer if the student fails a second time — and even then, pair it with a brief explanation of why.

Knowledge gap map

At the end of each Practice Mode session, generate a visual breakdown of topics the student got right vs. topics they struggled on. Store per-topic accuracy in the user’s Supabase record (linked to subject). On subsequent sessions, surface this map as a “where to focus” recommendation before the quiz starts. The adaptive difficulty system should also pull from this data when deciding which question types to weight more heavily.

Flashcard deck generation

When a student uploads notes, slides, or a syllabus, add a “Generate Flashcards” button. The AI extracts key terms and concepts and returns them as structured card data (front: term, back: definition + context snippet). Render as a flippable card UI within Practice Mode. Each card’s back side includes a “Why It Matters” or “Context Clue” sentence — not just the raw definition. This is the difference between memorization and understanding. Instruct the AI explicitly in the prompt to always include this context field.

Socratic audio teasers

Generate a short (2–3 minute) audio clip where two AI voices debate a concept from the student’s current study topic. The script is generated by Claude: instruct it to write a dialogue between two characters who disagree on an interpretation, and to end the clip with an unresolved question directed at the listener. Use a TTS API (e.g. ElevenLabs or browser SpeechSynthesis for a no-cost fallback) to render the script as audio. The clip intentionally does not resolve the debate — it ends with a prompt for the student to jump into chat and answer. Treat this as a session entry point, not a summary tool.

Grader / Reviewer Mode

Custom rubric upload for Teacher and Teacher Pro

The default rubric is the Texas 4/5-point writing scale. Teacher and Teacher Pro users can upload or paste a custom rubric before students submit work. Store custom rubrics in Supabase tied to the teacher’s account and class. When a student in that class uses Grader Mode, pull the teacher’s rubric and inject it into the grading system prompt instead of the default. The AI must evaluate the submission against the custom criteria and output the scorecard using the teacher’s own categories. Fall back to the default rubric if none has been set.

Tiers & Pricing

Annual billing option for Pro

Add an annual billing option on the Pro tier at a discount, targeting approximately $60–$70/year vs $7/month. Present both options on the upgrade screen with the annual price shown as a per-month equivalent (e.g. “$5.83/mo, billed annually”). Parents are the likely paying party and strongly prefer a single annual purchase over a recurring monthly charge. Implement via Stripe with two separate price IDs for the same product — one monthly, one annual. Cancellation and refund logic must handle both billing cycles.

Department tier (between Teacher Pro and Enterprise)

The gap between a $15–$20/mo Teacher Pro account and a $500+/mo Enterprise contract is a dead zone. Create a Department tier targeting groups of 5–10 teachers from the same school who sign up together. Price it at roughly $40–$60/mo for the group. One billing account covers all seats. Each teacher gets Teacher Pro-equivalent access. This tier is sold directly (not self-serve) during early rollout. It fills the gap for departments that want to adopt the tool without going through district procurement.

Ad System & Token Economics

Track token-to-API-cost ratio from day one

The token system (1 rewarded ad = 5 tokens, 1 extra session = 10 tokens) creates a direct dependency on AdSense CPMs, which fluctuate. 1. Auto-Generated Context Flashcards

Instead of forcing the student to build them manually, Socrify does the heavy lifting based on their study materials.

  • Smart Extraction: Students upload their notes, slides, or syllabus, and Socrify instantly generates a full deck of complete flashcards.
  • The Socrify Twist: Standard flashcards just give the definition. Socrify’s flashcards also include a brief "Context Clue" or "Why it Matters" snippet on the back of the card, helping students actually understand the term rather than just memorizing a string of words.

2. Instant Mindmaps & Concept Webs

Visual learners need to see the big picture. When a student needs to review an entire unit before an exam, give them the whole map.

  • One-Click Generation: Transform a chaotic 10-page Google Doc of lecture notes into a clean, structured visual mindmap.
  • Interactive Nodes: Students can click on any node in the mindmap to instantly open up a quick summary of that specific concept, making it a highly scannable dashboard for test prep.

3. Adaptive Quizzes with Socratic Feedback

This is where you bridge the gap between providing standard study tools and maintaining your core AI persona.

  • Test Your Knowledge: Socrify generates full, traditional multiple-choice or short-answer quizzes based on the student's study path or uploaded materials.
  • The Socrify Twist: If a student gets a question right, great! If they get it wrong, Socrify doesn't just highlight the correct answer. Instead, the AI steps in to quickly coach them on why their answer was wrong and guides them to the correct logic. It’s a standard quiz, but the grading is Socratic.
